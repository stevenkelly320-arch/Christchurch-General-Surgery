Endoscopy

Gastroscopy and colonoscopy in Christchurch

Half-day endoscopy under surgeon-administered sedation, with a direct pathway to treatment if anything is found. Gastroscopy

A gastroscopy examines the oesophagus, stomach and the first part of the small bowel using a thin flexible camera passed through the mouth while you are sedated. It is a short procedure, and you are home the same day. Common reasons for gastroscopy include:

  • Heartburn and reflux, and assessment before anti-reflux surgery
  • Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) or food sticking
  • Upper abdominal pain or persistent indigestion
  • Iron deficiency anaemia
  • Unexplained weight loss or vomiting

Colonoscopy

A colonoscopy examines the whole large bowel with a flexible camera, under sedation, after a bowel preparation the day before. Polyps can be removed and biopsies taken during the procedure. Common reasons for colonoscopy include:

  • Rectal bleeding
  • A change in bowel habit
  • Iron deficiency anaemia
  • Abdominal pain
  • A family history of bowel cancer, or surveillance after previous polyps

Why have endoscopy with a surgeon

Accurate diagnosis is the foundation of good treatment. Having your endoscopy within a surgical practice means that if the findings need an operation, gallbladder disease or a hernia found during assessment for example, the pathway to treatment is direct and within the one team.

Dr Grant Coulter is now in semi-retirement after 35 years of a highly respected career as a consultant general surgeon. Although he no longer performs surgery, he is available to see patients for consultations and he also performs gastroscopy and colonoscopy. If surgery is needed he refers you directly to Dr Kelly or Dr Flint.

What to expect on the day

Endoscopy is a half-day procedure performed under sedation administered by your surgeon, so you will need someone to drive you home and should not drive, work or make important decisions for the rest of the day. The rooms will send you written instructions, including fasting times and, for colonoscopy, the bowel preparation. Your endoscopist will discuss the findings with you afterwards, and any biopsy results are followed up with you and your GP.

Where to start

Send a request through this page or call the rooms on 03 375 4949. No GP referral is required, although one is welcome. Please tell the rooms if you take blood thinners or have diabetes, as your preparation may need adjusting.

Common questions

Questions patients ask about gastroscopy and colonoscopy.

Do I need a referral for a gastroscopy or colonoscopy?

No. Direct referrals from patients are accepted. A GP referral is helpful but not required.

Is endoscopy painful?

Both procedures are performed under sedation and most people remember little or nothing of them. You may feel bloated for a short time afterwards.

How do I prepare?

The rooms will send written instructions. Gastroscopy needs a period of fasting. Colonoscopy also needs a bowel preparation taken the day before to clear the bowel.

Who performs the procedure?

Dr Grant Coulter, Dr Steven Kelly and Dr Richard Flint all perform gastroscopy and colonoscopy. You can ask for a particular endoscopist when you book.

What happens if something is found?

Polyps can usually be removed and biopsies taken during the procedure. If a condition needing surgery is found, you will be seen by Dr Kelly or Dr Flint within the same practice.
