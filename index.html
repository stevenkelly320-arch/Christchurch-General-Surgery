How Dr Coulter works with the team

Dr Coulter is in semi-retirement and no longer performs surgery. If your consultation or endoscopy shows that surgery is needed, he refers you directly to Dr Kelly or Dr Flint, so your care stays within the one practice.

  • General surgery consultations
  • Assessment of abdominal pain
  • Gastroscopy and colonoscopy
  • Referral to Dr Kelly or Dr Flint for surgery
Do I need a GP referral?

No. All three surgeons accept direct referrals from patients. A GP referral is helpful because it brings your history and test results with you, but it is not required.

Who will I see for abdominal pain or endoscopy?

Any of the three surgeons. Dr Grant Coulter, a consultant general surgeon now in semi-retirement after a 35-year career, sees patients for consultations and performs gastroscopy and colonoscopy. He no longer performs surgery, so if surgery is needed he refers you to Dr Kelly or Dr Flint.

Where will my operation take place?

Private surgery is performed at St George's Hospital and Southern Cross Hospital in Christchurch. Both operating surgeons also work in the public system at Christchurch Hospital.

How long will I be in hospital?

Keyhole gallbladder surgery is usually one night. Anti-reflux and hiatal hernia surgery is usually one to two nights, with a return to work in about two weeks.

What tests happen before anti-reflux surgery?

Preoperative testing typically includes gastroscopy. Oesophageal pH testing and manometry are arranged when clinically indicated.

How do I enquire about weight loss surgery?

Bariatric surgery runs through Christchurch Weight Loss Surgery, a dedicated multidisciplinary programme. Programme details, pricing and self-referral are at cwls.co.nz.

How do GPs refer?

By Healthlink EDI cwlssurg, or by email to info@cgsurgery.co.nz. Urgent cases are seen promptly, and complex cancer cases are reviewed by a multidisciplinary board.
