What a hernia is

A hernia is a weakness or gap in the abdominal wall through which fat or bowel can push, producing a bulge. Hernias often ache or feel uncomfortable, especially when standing, lifting or coughing, and they do not heal on their own. They tend to enlarge slowly with time. Occasionally bowel becomes trapped in a hernia, which is painful and needs urgent treatment, so a hernia that suddenly becomes painful, hard or cannot be pushed back should be seen promptly.

Hernias we repair

  • Inguinal (groin) hernia, the most common type, repaired by keyhole or open surgery
  • Femoral hernia
  • Umbilical and paraumbilical hernia, repaired with sutures or mesh depending on size
  • Incisional hernia at the site of previous surgery
  • Recurrent hernia after previous repair
  • Complex abdominal wall reconstruction, including transversus abdominis release (TAR), for large or difficult hernias

Keyhole or open repair

Both surgeons perform laparoscopic (keyhole) and open hernia repair. Keyhole repair places mesh behind the abdominal wall through small incisions, which suits many groin hernias, hernias on both sides and recurrent hernias. Open repair through a single incision remains an excellent choice for other patients. The right technique depends on the type and size of the hernia, your previous surgery and your general health, and your surgeon will explain the recommendation at your consultation.

Complex and recurrent hernias are a particular focus. Abdominal wall reconstruction techniques such as TAR allow large incisional hernias to be repaired while restoring the function of the abdominal wall.

Recovery after hernia surgery

Many groin and umbilical hernia repairs are day-case or one-night procedures. Larger incisional repairs and abdominal wall reconstruction involve a longer stay. Most people are walking on the day of surgery and back to light activities within days. Heavy lifting and strenuous exercise are restricted for a period while the repair strengthens, and your surgeon will give you a clear timeline for driving, work and exercise.

Common questions

Questions patients ask about hernia surgery.

Can a hernia go away on its own?

No. A hernia is a physical gap in the abdominal wall and only surgery can close it. Small hernias that cause no symptoms can sometimes be watched, but most hernias enlarge with time.

Will mesh be used?

Most adult hernia repairs use mesh because it lowers the chance of the hernia coming back. Small umbilical hernias can sometimes be repaired with sutures alone. Your surgeon will discuss the options with you.

Keyhole or open: which is better?

Both are excellent operations in the right situation. Keyhole repair is often preferred for groin hernias on both sides and for recurrent hernias, while open repair suits many others. The recommendation depends on your hernia and your health.

How soon can I return to work?

Desk-based work is often possible within a week of a groin or umbilical repair. Physical work and heavy lifting take longer. Your surgeon will give you a specific timeline.

What if my hernia suddenly becomes very painful?

A hernia that becomes hard, very painful or cannot be pushed back may be trapped and needs urgent assessment. Contact the rooms or attend an emergency department.
