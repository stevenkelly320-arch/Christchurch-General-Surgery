Gallbladder surgery

Gallbladder surgery in Christchurch

Keyhole removal of the gallbladder (laparoscopic cholecystectomy) for gallstones, performed by Dr Richard Flint and Dr Steven Kelly at St George's and Southern Cross Hospitals. Most patients stay one night and are back to normal activities in about a week.

When gallbladder surgery is recommended

Gallstones are common and often cause no trouble. Surgery is recommended when stones cause symptoms or complications, because once a gallbladder has caused pain it usually keeps doing so. Typical reasons for referral include:

  • Biliary colic: episodes of pain in the upper right abdomen or below the breastbone, often after meals and sometimes felt in the back or right shoulder
  • Cholecystitis: inflammation or infection of the gallbladder
  • Stones that have moved into the bile duct, causing jaundice or infection
  • Gallstone pancreatitis
  • Some gallbladder polyps and other gallbladder conditions

If you have pain, nausea after fatty food, or an ultrasound showing gallstones, you can contact the rooms directly. A GP referral is welcome but not required.

What the operation involves

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy removes the whole gallbladder through four small incisions using a camera and fine instruments. It is performed under general anaesthetic and both surgeons use keyhole techniques as the standard approach, which means smaller scars, less pain and a faster recovery than open surgery.

Stones in the bile duct

If stones have passed into the bile duct they can usually be cleared laparoscopically during the same operation, so the gallbladder and the bile duct are dealt with together in one anaesthetic rather than as separate procedures.

Between them, Dr Flint and Dr Kelly have performed more than 10,000 laparoscopic procedures. Both are FRACS-qualified and fellowship-trained at internationally renowned centres.

Recovery after gallbladder surgery

Most patients stay one night in hospital and are back to normal activities within about a week. You will be given clear written instructions about wound care, diet, activity and pain relief, and a direct line to the rooms if you have any concerns. Your surgeon will tell you when it is safe to drive and return to work, which for most people is within the first week or two.

Living without a gallbladder does not change how most people eat or digest food. Bile still flows from the liver to the bowel; it simply is no longer stored on the way.

Common questions

Questions patients ask about gallbladder surgery.

Do I need a GP referral for gallbladder surgery?

No. Dr Flint and Dr Kelly accept direct referrals from patients. A GP referral is helpful because it brings your history and scan results with you, but it is not required.

How long will I be in hospital?

Laparoscopic gallbladder surgery is usually a one-night stay. Most people are back to normal activities within about a week.

What if I have stones in the bile duct as well?

Bile duct stones can usually be removed laparoscopically in the same operation as the gallbladder, so both problems are treated in one anaesthetic.

Is keyhole surgery always possible?

Almost always. Both surgeons use laparoscopic techniques as standard. Very occasionally, if the gallbladder is severely inflamed or scarred, a larger incision is safer. Your surgeon will discuss this with you beforehand.

Can I live normally without a gallbladder?

Yes. The gallbladder stores bile but is not essential. Most people notice no long-term change to their diet or digestion after surgery.
